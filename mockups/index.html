UptimeHealth · OKR Tracker

Three UI directions

Each one keeps 100% of current functionality — every button, permission, modal and field survives. What changes is layout, density, and how quickly someone can answer “what’s off track and who owns it?”

The shared structural fix: today only one objective is visible at a time (tabs), and progress is shown as a raw percentage with no sense of whether that’s good for late August. All three directions show the whole portfolio and add a pace marker — the expected progress for today, 57% into Q3.
Direction A

Command Center

Dense · neutral · dark-mode · Linear-inspired

Everything on one screen at maximum information density. Objectives are collapsible rows, key results are table rows with inline sparklines, and actions appear on hover so the page stays quiet. Built for someone who lives in this tool daily and wants to scan eleven key results in one glance.

Strengths
  • Highest information density
  • True dark mode
  • Scales past 20 objectives
  • Feels like modern dev tooling
Trade-offs
  • Least “executive presentation” feel
  • Descriptions and notes are one click away
  • Hover-reveal is weaker on touch

Direction B

Executive Portfolio

Airy · confident · Lattice / Viva Goals-inspired

Big typography, generous spacing, and a scoreboard up top with health segments and quarter timeline. Every objective is a full card that always shows its key results in a clean table with current, target, and pace. Designed for the quarterly review meeting — you can share your screen and nobody has to ask what they’re looking at.

Strengths
  • Best for exec review / screen-share
  • Health and pace are unmissable
  • Descriptions get real space
  • Activity rail stays visible
Trade-offs
  • More scrolling with many objectives
  • Lower density per screen

Direction C

Focus Board

Two-pane · closest to today · Notion-inspired

Keeps your current “one objective at a time” mental model but fixes its blind spot: a left rail lists every objective with its health, so you never lose the portfolio view while working in one. Drag-to-reorder still lives in the rail. Notes and History move into tabs so key result cards stay short. Smallest change for your users to absorb.

Strengths
  • Lowest retraining cost
  • Portfolio + detail simultaneously
  • Keeps drag-to-reorder
  • Cards stop growing forever
Trade-offs
  • Can’t compare two objectives side by side
  • Needs a real mobile fallback

Improvements common to all three

  • Pace marker — a tick on every progress bar showing expected progress for today (day 52 of 92). “50%” means something different in July than in September.
  • Derived health — On Track / At Risk / Off Track computed from progress vs. time elapsed, replacing the flat purple bar where everything looked the same.
  • No tabs — the whole portfolio is visible; objectives are never hidden behind a click.
  • Neutral palette with one accent — the tri-colour pastel gradient is replaced with a calm neutral so the red/amber/green status colours actually stand out.
  • Quieter secondary actions — Edit / Move / Delete stop competing with Submit Update.
  • Tabular numerals — figures line up in columns instead of jittering.
